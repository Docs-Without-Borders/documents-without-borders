Other Countries, Territories & Entities Not Counted in the 195

Educational legal reference for the global notary community. These entities are neither Hague nor Non-Hague members. They cannot receive a Hague Apostille directly under the 1961 Convention, and they are not listed as standard non-member states for consular legalization because their legal status is territory, disputed, de facto, or special administrative region.

⚖️ Important for Notaries: For documents from these places, authentication goes through the administering power or parent country. Example: Puerto Rico → US Apostille, Hong Kong → China Apostille (since China joined 7 Nov 2023), Greenland → Denmark Apostille.
There are 195 internationally recognized countries — 193 UN Members + 2 Observers (Holy See & Palestine) — with 130 contracting parties to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention as of June 30, 2026. The entities below are additional territories, disputed, and de facto states NOT counted in the 195 and are neither Hague nor Non-Hague members.

6️⃣ Off-Limits, Do Not Visit & Restricted Territories — Cannot Be Visited

These are real places on Earth that are legally off-limits to the public, protected by law, military, or extreme danger. For notaries and legal purposes, no civil registry, no competent authority, and no Apostille can be obtained from them directly — documents would go through the administering country.

For Documents Without Borders Global Members: If a client claims a birth certificate or document from North Sentinel Island, Snake Island, Surtsey, etc. — it does not exist. These places have no civil registry, no hospital, no government office issuing public documents. Any such document is fraudulent. Authentic documents for Andaman Islands come from India (Hague Member since 2005), for Ilha da Queimada Grande from Brazil (Hague Member since 2016), etc.

7️⃣ Why This Matters for Notaries & Legalization

These territories are neither Hague nor Non-Hague members — meaning you will not find them on the official HCCH 130 list, and you will not find them as standard non-member countries requiring direct embassy legalization.

- Territory: Legally part of another country. Uses parent country's Apostille. (e.g., Puerto Rico uses US Apostille)
- Disputed: Two or more governments claim it. Authentication depends on which government controls the area where document was issued.
- De Facto State: Controls territory but lacks UN recognition. Documents often not accepted internationally without third-country authentication.

Golden Rule for DWB Global Members: Always ask: Who issues the civil registry/birth certificate? Who is the competent authority for Apostille? If no competent authority, authenticate through the administering power.
